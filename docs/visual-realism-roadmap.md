# Visual realism roadmap: a grounded city transit simulator

Research date: 2026-10-09. This note supports a GTA V-like **grounded, populated city presentation** for the existing transit simulator. It does not describe Rockstar's engine internals or promise AAA asset density in a browser. Priorities below are engineering recommendations inferred from the documented capabilities, rather than measured improvements to this repository.

## Recommendation

Build a convincing 250–400 m street/station scene around Geroksruhe first, then extend the same reusable kit along the 2.44 km U15 corridor to Eugensplatz. The largest current visual gap is the scene's material, silhouette and street detail. The vehicle already has considerably more visual detail than its surroundings. A citywide dense mesh or a renderer migration should follow demonstrated requirements, rather than precede this showcase.

Assumed target: a desktop browser at 60 fps, with optional lower quality presets. This is a planning assumption pending target hardware selection. New rendering and scenery features should work in both simulator and Live 3D through shared engine systems and provider boundaries.

## Suggested implementation order

Effort labels below describe relative scope, not calendar estimates. Art/reference work is part of the effort.

| Priority | Concrete work | Why it comes here | Effort / cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Add frame CPU/GPU timings where supported, draw/triangle/shadow counts, approximate texture residency and streaming/decode timing. Define Low/Medium/High presets and fixed-camera baselines. | New texture and postprocessing work needs a measured ceiling. Existing headless frame timings do not establish target-hardware GPU headroom. | Small–medium; telemetry overhead should be switchable. |
| 1 | Shared asphalt, paving, ballast, grass, stone/stucco and roof material kit; façade/window atlas; deterministic material variation; UVs measured in meters. Include bounded texture loading and compressed runtime formats. | Covers much of every frame and makes existing geometry respond believably to the existing lights. | Medium; texture residency, shader cost and authoring. |
| 2 | Geroksruhe street/station slice: correct curbs/levels, retaining walls, roof shapes, track surface, catenary/poles, fences, drains, signage, benches, shelters and parked cars; small realistic vegetation kit. | Establishes scale, place and foreground silhouettes. Reuse generic props; author geographically verified exceptions through a city provider. | Large; data/reference/art and geometry. |
| 3 | Shared lighting calibration and optional composer: coherent sky/environment/exposure, stable sun/contact shadows, restrained reduced-resolution AO, appropriate anti-aliasing and mild lamp bloom. | Improves depth after materials and geometry supply meaningful surfaces. Compare each pass independently. | Medium; screen resolution and extra shadow views can dominate GPU cost. |
| 4 | Crop DGM1 and LoD2 for U15; convert offline to small terrain/building tiles, preserve vertical reference metadata, add façade materials and authored exceptions. | Improves actual relief and roof shapes where the current source is visibly wrong. Validate local coverage before wider rollout. | Large; geodata conversion, seams, source alignment and cache budget. |
| 5 | Ground traffic/pedestrians consistently; add believable lane/headway/signal/crossing behavior and nearby animation LOD. Add weather-aware road/roof wetness and puddle masks. | Removes moving contact errors and makes the street feel populated and reactive. | Medium–large; local state, animation and transparency. |
| 6 | Validated cab anchors, camera clearance and damping, compact simulator HUD, spatial motor/rolling/braking/door/announcement sounds, then near vehicle/cab polish. | Makes the game-quality scene feel like a simulator being driven. Preserve readable signals, stop markers and instruments. | Medium–large; animation/audio assets and usability checks. |

The first reviewable visual milestone is priorities 0–2 on one street/station block, with a calibrated existing lighting setup. Extend that kit to Eugensplatz only after comparing the same camera frames and a moving run. Optional full-screen effects, detailed reflections, dense crowds and wider geography are subsequent measured additions.

Use 0–150 m as an initial detailed region, 150–600 m for reduced detail, and a simplified distant skyline. Refine these distances using screen size and performance. Detailed infrastructure should also stream in spatial chunks; the existing whole-route rail/station geometry would otherwise keep growing in cost as the asset detail increases. Prefetch in the direction of travel and maintain small cache/concurrency limits, cancellation, LOD hysteresis and complete disposal.

For the small showcase, a manifest of tiled GLBs and generic scenery/terrain providers is sufficient to trial the data and authored assets. Evaluate 3D Tiles only when broader coverage makes hierarchy and screen-space error selection worthwhile. Runtime rendering belongs to the browser engine; geodata preparation belongs to tools/import commands. Keep deterministic route movement, braking, stops and dwell in Go/WASM.

### Completion criteria for a showcase

- Compare Geroksruhe, a verified midpoint street/slope, and Eugensplatz from cab and chase cameras, plus a continuous corridor run. Use fixed resolution, season, hour, weather, route distance and random seeds.
- Surfaces show distinct material response at correct scale; nearby façades/roofs and vegetation have convincing silhouettes; curbs/walls/platforms meet the terrain; actors remain in contact with their road/footway. Signals and stop targets remain readable in daytime, rain, sunset, night and portals.
- No distracting LOD/seam flicker, blocking tile/decode stalls, camera penetration or unbounded growth on a repeated drive and view switch. Check simulator and Live 3D with the same assets and presentation settings.
- Establish the 60 fps target on named desktop hardware at 1080p using CPU and GPU measurements; record p50/p95, streaming stalls, draw calls and memory. A proposed acceptance target is p95 frame time at or below 16.7 ms after loading, without degrading contact/readability. Adjust quality for a measured 30 fps fallback if desired.
- Retain source/asset licenses and visible attribution. Public GIS data supplies geometry/reference; every external texture, model and sound also needs suitable redistribution rights.

## Rendering improvements available in the current stack

The repository pins Three.js 0.184.0. Its installed addons already include CSM, GTAOPass, SMAAPass, OutputPass and KTX2Loader; GLTFLoader exposes meshopt and KTX2 decoder hooks. No renderer migration is required to trial these features.

The repository audit confirms existing ACES tone mapping, sRGB output, antialiasing, an environment map, sun shadows and advanced vehicle materials. The recommendations therefore concern calibration, scene materials/detail and optional additional passes; they do not imply those foundations are missing. Three.js can automatically prefilter equirectangular environment maps, so an explicit PMREM call is not itself a quality improvement.

| Change | Visual purpose | Practical constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calibrated PBR material library | Asphalt, concrete, stone, stucco, roof tiles, steel and glass should react differently to the same sun/sky. Use small reusable base-color/normal/roughness texture sets, consistent real-world UV scale, and restrained surface variation. | `MeshStandardMaterial` implements metallic/roughness PBR and recommends an environment map. Normal maps change shading, not geometry; curbs and roof silhouettes still need geometry. [Material documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html). |
| Environment lighting and a coherent sky | Reflections and indirect sky illumination make vehicles, windows and metal read as real surfaces. Match the environment's direction and brightness to the visible sun. | PMREM filters an environment for different material roughness levels. Generate/load environments on a time/weather change rather than every frame. [PMREM documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PMREMGenerator.html). |
| Camera-centered sun shadows | Contact shadows under vehicles and readable building/tree shadows give the city depth. Start with a tightly fitted sun shadow region; trial two or three cascades if its range remains insufficient. | CSM has one shadow-casting directional light per cascade, requires material setup and frame updates, and exposes distance/fading controls. Limit detailed shadow casters to a near region. [CSM documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/CSM.html). Shadow maps redraw casters per light; shadowed point lights require six views. [Shadow manual](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/shadows.html). |
| Restrained ambient occlusion | Darken roof/wall corners, wheel contact and platform edges so objects feel attached to their surroundings. | GTAO is documented as higher quality and more expensive than SSAO. Trial a reduced-resolution pass with an off switch; compare moving-camera stability as well as still images. [GTAOPass documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GTAOPass.html). |
| Consistent HDR/output pipeline | Preserve bright sky/headlamp highlights while keeping shaded streets readable; reduce jagged rail and cable edges. | Three.js works in Linear-sRGB and outputs display sRGB. Color textures use sRGB; normal/roughness data must not be treated as color. [Color management](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/color-management.html). Use one final OutputPass for tone mapping/conversion; SMAA belongs before it. [OutputPass](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/OutputPass.html), [SMAA](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/SMAAPass.html). |
| Selective physical materials | Clearcoat can help vehicle paint and rain-wet surfaces; reflective glass can improve the cab and shelters. | Physical material features increase per-pixel cost. Apply advanced transmission/clearcoat to a small number of near assets, retaining cheaper materials elsewhere. [Physical material documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html). |

Recommendation: texture and light one representative station/street block first. Postprocessing has limited value when the visible world still consists mainly of plain extrusions. Avoid strong bloom, heavy depth of field or motion blur in the normal driving view because the driver must read signals and stopping points; this is a simulator usability judgment, not a documented rendering limitation. A small bloom contribution for bright lamps can be evaluated later. Three.js's bloom pass provides luminance threshold, radius and strength controls. [Bloom documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/UnrealBloomPass.html).

## Stuttgart data that can improve the U15 showcase

| Dataset | What it supplies | What it still needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LGL DGM1 / DGM025 | Bare-earth terrain, excluding vegetation and artificial structures. LGL offers 1 m and 0.25 m open products; current standard format is GeoTIFF and heights use NHN/DHHN2016. | Crop and preprocess the corridor; verify XY projection, pixel centers, vertical datum and source date. Keep track slabs, station platforms, retaining walls and bridges as designed infrastructure. High source resolution does not require uniformly dense runtime terrain. [LGL DGM description](https://www.lgl-bw.de/Produkte/3D-Produkte/Digitale-Gelaendemodelle/). |
| LGL LoD2 CityGML | Building footprints/heights with standardized pitched/hip/mansard/etc. roof forms; approximate stated height accuracy is 1 m. | The official product excludes facade textures and roof appendages, and complex roofs can have gross errors. Convert only intersecting buildings offline into tiled renderable assets, then add reusable facades/windows and authored landmark exceptions. It improves skyline and roof silhouette, not finished street-level realism by itself. [LGL LoD2 description](https://www.lgl-bw.de/Produkte/3D-Produkte/3D-Gebaeudemodelle/LoD2/). |
| LGL DOP20 | Statewide 20 cm orthophotos, TrueDOP since 2020, supplied as 1 km units in GeoTIFF/TIFF. Useful for placement verification, real ground color and distant scenery. | An orthophoto is a top-down image: it cannot supply building facades or vertical retaining walls. It also contains captured illumination and visible roofs/vegetation, so blindly draping it under separately rendered objects can produce duplicated shapes and lighting. Use selectively for backdrop/reference or preprocess ground masks. [LGL DOP20 description](https://www.lgl-bw.de/Produkte/Luftbildprodukte/DOP20/). |

LGL's open-data page specifies Datenlizenz Deutschland – Namensnennung 2.0 and the visible attribution **“Datenquelle: LGL, www.lgl-bw.de, dl-de/by-2-0”**. Record the dataset/version and retain this alongside existing OSM/OpenFreeMap/Mapzen credits. [Official access and attribution page](https://www.lgl-bw.de/Produkte/Open-Data/index.html). Preserve the generic city/provider boundary so other cities can use equivalent datasets and procedural fallback coverage.

The most promising local trial is Geroksruhe → Eugensplatz: compare DGM1 terrain, LoD2 roof shapes, and one authored near-track scene containing retaining walls, sidewalk/curbs, realistic track bed, poles/wires, station furniture and a small regional facade/vegetation kit. This is an inferred prioritization for that steep corridor. The earlier [terrain investigation](terrain-precision-research.md) already established why lateral relief and independent infrastructure heights matter.

## Asset density requires a streaming budget

Use authored detail where the driver can see it, then progressively reduce it. Suggested initial bands for measurement are 0–150 m detailed street/track assets, 150–600 m simpler facades and vegetation, and farther skyline/terrain masses. These are trial distances, not validated optimal values. Camera field of view and screen-space size should eventually refine them.

- Reuse geometry/materials for trees, lamps, benches, poles, parked cars and repeated facade parts through instancing. Three.js documents InstancedMesh as reducing draw calls for repeated meshes. Partition instances into spatial chunks to preserve useful culling. [Instancing documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html).
- Author at least near/mid/far versions and use transition hysteresis to prevent LOD flicker. Three.js provides distance-based LOD and hysteresis. [LOD documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LOD.html).
- Compress render textures as KTX2/Basis and mesh assets with meshopt when appropriate. GLTFLoader supports the relevant extensions, but decoders must be configured and decoded image resources explicitly disposed. [GLTFLoader](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html), [KTX2Loader](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/KTX2Loader.html).
- For a small authored corridor, a simple tiled GLB manifest may be sufficient. For wider LoD2 coverage, evaluate 3D Tiles rather than loading an entire CityGML/city mesh. OGC's format uses a hierarchical structure for streaming geospatial content. [3D Tiles standard](https://www.ogc.org/standards/3dtiles/). NASA-AMMOS's Three.js renderer provides LOD streaming and hard cache caps; refinement can stop when required tiles exceed the cache budget. [First-party implementation](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS).

Texture residency needs its own limit. Calculated example: a 1 km square at DOP20 resolution is 5,000 × 5,000 pixels; expanding it to RGBA8 consumes about 95 MiB before mipmaps. A 2,048² RGBA8 texture with its full mip chain uses about 21 MiB, so three uncompressed PBR maps already consume about 64 MiB. Keep high detail cropped and streamed; source-file JPEG/PNG size is not the runtime texture cost.

Record CPU/GPU frame time, draw calls, visible triangles, shadow draws, resident texture bytes, cache bytes, worker/decode times and scene-loading stalls at fixed U15 cameras and while driving. Compare clear daytime, overcast, sunset and night. Prefer the next change that removes a visible defect while staying inside a measured target-device budget. A 60 Hz frame is 16.7 ms and a 30 Hz frame is 33.3 ms; these are target budgets, not achieved results.
